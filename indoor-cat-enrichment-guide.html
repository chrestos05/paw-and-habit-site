Indoor Cat Enrichment: A Practical Guide

Indoor cats can thrive when their homes support natural behaviors: hunting, foraging, climbing, scratching, exploring, and resting safely.

The six enrichment categories

  1. Hunting and pouncing: Use wand toys and prey-like movement. Let your cat catch the toy at the end.
  2. Foraging: Offer part of a meal in a puzzle feeder or hide small portions in easy-to-find places.
  3. Vertical territory: Add a stable perch, shelf, cat tree, or window spot with a clear escape route.
  4. Scratching: Provide a sturdy surface near sleeping areas or places your cat already scratches.
  5. Sensory variety: Rotate paper, cardboard, safe scents, textures, and window views rather than leaving everything out at once.
  6. Social interaction: Offer short, positive play or calm attention. Let the cat choose distance and duration.

Start with ten minutes

Choose one category, set it up with items you already own, and observe. If your cat walks away, reduce the difficulty or try a different location. Enrichment should be voluntary and rewarding, not forced.

Safety basics

  • Supervise new toys and DIY puzzles at first.
  • Remove strings, small parts, or sharp edges that could be swallowed.
  • Secure climbing furniture and keep food puzzles suitable for the individual cat.
  • Stop if your cat shows distress. Consult a veterinarian about sudden health or behavior changes.
